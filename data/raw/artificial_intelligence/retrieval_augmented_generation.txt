Retrieval-Augmented Generation, commonly known as RAG, is an approach that combines information retrieval systems with large language models to improve the accuracy and reliability of generated responses. Traditional language models generate answers based on patterns learned during training, but they may not have access to recent information or private organizational knowledge. RAG addresses this limitation by retrieving relevant information from external sources before generating an answer.

A typical RAG system consists of several main components. The first component is the document processing pipeline, where raw documents are collected, cleaned, and divided into smaller meaningful sections called chunks. Chunking is an important step because the entire document is usually too large to process efficiently during retrieval. Well-designed chunks should preserve enough context while remaining specific enough to match user questions accurately.

After documents are divided into chunks, each chunk is converted into a numerical representation called an embedding. Embeddings capture semantic meaning by representing text as vectors in a high-dimensional space. Similar concepts are placed closer together, allowing the system to search for relevant information based on meaning rather than only matching exact keywords.

The retrieval stage uses a vector search system to compare the embedding of a user's question with stored document embeddings. The system identifies the most relevant chunks and provides them as additional context to the language model. The language model then uses this retrieved information together with the user's question to generate a more accurate response.

One of the important advantages of RAG systems is that they allow organizations to use their own knowledge sources without retraining a large language model. For example, a company can connect internal documentation, technical manuals, research papers, or customer support information to a language model and create a specialized question-answering system.

Building a reliable RAG system requires attention to multiple engineering factors. Document loading, chunking strategy, embedding model selection, vector storage, retrieval quality, and response generation all affect the final performance. A weak retrieval step can cause a powerful language model to produce inaccurate answers because the model receives poor context.

In real-world applications, RAG systems are used in enterprise search, knowledge assistants, customer support platforms, research tools, and document analysis systems. Production-level RAG solutions often include additional features such as monitoring, evaluation pipelines, access control, data updates, and scalable storage systems to ensure consistent performance.

The Transformer architecture is one of the most important developments in modern artificial intelligence, especially in the field of natural language processing. Before Transformers, many language models relied on sequential architectures such as recurrent neural networks, which processed text step by step. These approaches had limitations when handling long sequences because remembering relationships between distant words was difficult.

The key innovation of the Transformer architecture is the attention mechanism. Attention allows the model to evaluate the importance of different words in a sequence and understand how they relate to each other. Instead of processing information only from previous steps, the model can consider the entire input sequence and identify meaningful connections between different parts of the text.

The self-attention mechanism enables each word representation to interact with other words in the same sequence. For example, in a sentence with ambiguous words, attention helps the model understand the context by considering surrounding information. This ability significantly improved language understanding and made Transformers suitable for large-scale language models.

A Transformer model usually consists of encoder and decoder components. The encoder focuses on understanding and representing input information, while the decoder generates new output based on the learned representations. Some modern models use only one part of this architecture depending on their purpose. For example, encoder-based models are often used for understanding tasks, while decoder-based models are widely used for text generation.

Large language models are built by training Transformer architectures on massive collections of text data. During training, the model learns statistical patterns, relationships between concepts, and structures of human language. However, the model does not store information like a traditional database. Instead, knowledge is represented through learned parameters that influence how the model generates responses.

The success of Transformers has expanded artificial intelligence applications beyond simple text processing. These models are now used in translation systems, coding assistants, search systems, document analysis, and retrieval-augmented generation applications. In industrial systems, Transformers are often combined with other components such as databases, retrieval systems, and external tools to create more reliable and specialized solutions.

Despite their powerful capabilities, Transformer-based models have challenges. They require significant computational resources, large amounts of training data, and careful evaluation. Issues such as incorrect generation, outdated knowledge, and high operating costs have encouraged researchers and engineers to design systems that combine language models with external knowledge sources.
